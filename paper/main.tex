\begin{abstract}
Every evaluation run of a vision--language model already produces
per-question artifacts---predictions, judged correctness, metadata---that
leaderboards reduce to a single score and then discard. We treat those
artifacts as a free audit of the benchmark itself, and add an audit layer to
VLMEvalKit: six detectors that run as post-processing over saved outputs,
plus one cheap text-only rerun with the images stripped, emitting a
per-question report in a single command. The layer reports what survives a
null model rather than raw detector counts, attaching to each finding its
excess over an item-matched permutation null, a confidence interval, and its
stability across model pools. Auditing MMStar---a benchmark curated
specifically so that the image is indispensable---with three models, we find
that on a fifth of its questions the pool gains nothing from the image, and
that 3.2 percentage points of that share exceed the null ($p=0.001$),
concentrated in the mathematics and science categories and absent from
coarse perception. A second detector flags 134 questions on which every
model that answered in parseable form picked the same non-gold option, and
these peak in exactly the
perception categories where the first signal disappears, so the two
pathologies are separable from the same artifacts. Replacing the LLM judge
with exact string matching changes no conclusion, and every scoring
discrepancy between the two traces to one model whose free-form answers a
parser cannot read. On a binary yes/no benchmark the detectors measurably
fall out of calibration---a scope boundary we measure and report rather than
leave implicit. The audit layer, the prediction artifacts and the generated
reports accompany this submission.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

MMStar was built by filtering candidate questions so that the image is
indispensable: items solvable by blind language models were removed by
construction \citep{mmstar2024}. Run three VLMs on it twice---once as
intended, once with every image stripped from the prompt---and align the two
runs question by question. On 303 of 1{,}500 questions (20.2\%) the pool is
exactly as accurate without the image as with it. Most of that share is
arithmetic rather than pathology: even blind answers unrelated to the items
tie full-run accuracy sometimes, and an item-matched permutation null puts
that baseline at 17.0\%. The remaining $+3.2$\,pp ($p=0.001$) is signal, and
it is not spread evenly---it sits in the two categories where world
knowledge substitutes for looking at the picture. Finding this required no
new benchmark, no annotation campaign, and almost no extra compute:
everything except one text-only rerun was already sitting in the evaluation
toolkit's output directory.

That is the argument of this paper. A growing literature documents
pathologies of multimodal benchmarks: items solvable without the image
\citep{goyal2017vqa,mmstar2024,mmevalpro2024,datbench2026}, wrong gold
labels \citep{northcutt2021pervasive,mmluredux2024,vendrow2025platinum},
position and selection biases in multiple choice
\citep{zheng2024selectors,mmbench2024,mmdetect2024}, and sensitivity of
reported scores to the extraction protocol \citep{rosenthal2025unexplored}.
The standard responses are to build cleaner replacement benchmarks
\citep{mmstar2024,naturalbench2024,vendrow2025platinum,datbench2026} or
standalone flaw-mining pipelines \citep{fantasticbugs2025,benchmarker2026}.
Both treat auditing as a separate research project with its own inference
budget. We take an infrastructure view instead: the artifacts that toolkits
such as VLMEvalKit \citep{vlmevalkit2024} and LMMs-Eval \citep{lmmseval2024}
persist on every run are already sufficient to audit the benchmark as a side
effect of evaluating on it.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{An audit layer for an evaluation toolkit rather than a
  standalone pipeline.} Six benchmark-pathology detectors consume the
  per-question files VLMEvalKit already writes; the only added inference is
  a text-only rerun, implemented centrally so that local and API models are
  stripped of visual content identically (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{A way of reading detector output that does not overstate
  it.} A raw flag count is not interpretable, because part of it is produced
  by marginal accuracies alone. We report instead the excess over an
  item-matched permutation null, with interval estimates, leave-one-model-out
  ranges and per-category strata, and show that the excess is the
  pool-stable quantity while the raw share is not
  (Section~\ref{sec:method-stats}).
  \item \textbf{A case study that separates two pathologies inside a curated
  benchmark.} On MMStar, text-solvable residue and label-error candidates
  are both present, are recovered from the same artifacts, and occupy
  opposite ends of the benchmark's own category taxonomy
  (Sections~\ref{sec:res-textonly}--\ref{sec:res-consensus}).
  \item \textbf{An account of the audit's own validity and limits.} We
  quantify how much of the audit depends on an LLM judge, attribute the
  residual dependence to a single model's output format, and identify a
  format---binary yes/no items---on which the detectors demonstrably lose
  calibration (Sections~\ref{sec:res-judge}--\ref{sec:res-binary}).
\end{enumerate}

We do not claim to discover these pathologies; blind-solvability and label
errors in VLM benchmarks are documented at scale by concurrent work
\citep{datbench2026,visualignorance2026}. Our claim is narrower: these
checks can run inside the evaluation infrastructure, from artifacts it
already saves, with uncertainty attached---so that every evaluation run can
double as a benchmark audit.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Benchmark pathologies.} Language priors in visual question
answering were established by \citet{goyal2017vqa}; MMStar
\citep{mmstar2024} and MMEvalPro \citep{mmevalpro2024} showed that blind
language models score far above chance on multimodal multiple-choice suites;
concurrent work probes the visual dependence of existing suites through blur
ablations \citep{visualignorance2026} or phrasing-controlled items
\citep{seeorguess2026}. Label errors destabilize rankings
\citep{northcutt2021pervasive}; re-annotation efforts include MMLU-Redux
\citep{mmluredux2024} and platinum benchmarks \citep{vendrow2025platinum},
and \citet{infodensity2025} report a fallibility analysis covering MMStar.
Position and selection bias in multiple choice is established for language
models \citep{zheng2024selectors}, handled in MMBench through CircularEval
\citep{mmbench2024}, and reused as a contamination probe
\citep{mmdetect2024}. \citet{kim2025correlated} show that model errors
correlate across vendors and scales, which caps what any cross-model
consensus can establish; we return to this in
Sections~\ref{sec:res-consensus} and~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Audit pipelines and toolkits.} Fantastic Bugs
\citep{fantasticbugs2025} mines response patterns to flag invalid questions
in nine text benchmarks; BenchMarker \citep{benchmarker2026} applies an
education-inspired rubric with LLM judges to text multiple-choice
benchmarks; DatBench \citep{datbench2026} audits 33 VLM datasets and
releases a cleaned suite. Our deliverable differs: not a cleaned dataset and
not a standalone pipeline, but audit machinery embedded in a mainstream
toolkit, reusable on any benchmark--model combination from artifacts the
toolkit already saves. To our knowledge no such layer exists for VLMEvalKit
or LMMs-Eval \citep{lmmseval2024}. Redundancy analyses over existing score
matrices \citep{redundancy2025} share the reuse-the-exhaust spirit for a
different question. On the measurement side, \citet{rosenthal2025unexplored}
document protocol sensitivity of VQA accuracy; we extend that question from
accuracy numbers to audit conclusions.

\subsection{Inputs and the blind-run protocol}
\label{sec:method-inputs}

The layer consumes the per-question result files of $n$ models on one
benchmark, each with a raw prediction, the gold answer, and optionally a
judged correctness flag. A \emph{blind run} repeats inference with all
visual content removed from every prompt, text untouched, implemented once
inside the toolkit so that local and API models are treated identically;
blind artifacts get a \texttt{\_blind} suffix and sit beside the full run.
All frames are aligned on the dataset-level question id---never on row
order---and restricted to the shared id set.

Two rules protect validity. First, model answers are never back-filled from
gold labels, whatever the judge decided; an earlier version of our own
pipeline made exactly this mistake, and repairing it multiplied the
unanimous-conflict count by eleven (Section~\ref{sec:res-selfaudit}).
Second, predictions from which no option letter can be parsed become an
abstention sentinel that is excluded from consensus votes rather than
silently counted as wrong.

\subsection{Detectors}
\label{sec:method-detectors}

Table~\ref{tab:detectors} summarizes the six detectors. Consensus,
agreement, position-distribution and distractor detectors parse option
letters from raw predictions and never consult a judge; visual dependency
and correctness agreement consume judged labels and are re-checked under
exact matching in Section~\ref{sec:res-judge}.

  \begin{tabular}{lllc}

    \bottomrule

For visual dependency, each question is categorized from model-averaged full
accuracy $a^F_q$ and blind accuracy $a^B_q$: \emph{visual-dependent}
($a^F_q \ge 0.99$ and $a^B_q \le 0.01$), \emph{conflicting}
($a^B_q > a^F_q + \delta$, the image hurts), \emph{visual supplement}
($a^F_q - a^B_q \ge \delta$), and \emph{text-only} otherwise, with
$\delta = 0.2$. With $n=3$ models the accuracies live on the grid
$\{0, 1/3, 2/3, 1\}$, so text-only reduces to exact equality
$a^F_q = a^B_q$ and carries no tunable threshold. The visual-dependent
share, in contrast, is threshold-fragile---22.5\% under the strict
definition against 54.8\% under a majority definition
(Appendix~\ref{app:sweep})---which is why we never headline it. Consensus
conflicts are tiered: \emph{unanimous} (every parseable answer is the same
non-gold option), \emph{high} (all models disagree with the gold label),
\emph{medium} (a qualified majority disagrees).

\subsection{Audit statistics: report the excess, not the share}
\label{sec:method-stats}

A raw text-only share is easy to over-read. Even if blind answers were drawn
independently of the items, some questions would tie full-run accuracy by
chance, and that baseline moves with the pool's marginal accuracy---across
our two- and three-model pools the raw share swings by 14.2\,pp. The layer
therefore reports the share's \emph{excess over a permutation null}: blind
score vectors are shuffled across questions, with the same permutation
applied to every model, which preserves each model's marginal blind accuracy
and the inter-model correlation structure while destroying only the
per-question full-to-blind pairing. We use 999 permutations; the one-sided
$p$-value uses the standard $(g{+}1)/(N{+}1)$ correction, so the smallest
reportable value is $p=0.001$. The same module attaches Wilson 95\%
intervals to headline proportions (item-level uncertainty),
leave-one-model-out pools (pool sensitivity), a split-half reproducibility
check, and a stratification by official dataset categories (localization and
internal replication). All of it is post-processing; no model is queried
again.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:method-protocol}

\textbf{Benchmarks.} MMStar \citep{mmstar2024}: 1{,}500 four-option
multiple-choice items curated for vision-indispensability, with six official
categories of 250 items each. HallusionBench \citep{hallusionbench2024}: 951
yes/no items built around visual illusions and language priors, used here as
a deliberate out-of-format probe of the detectors themselves.

\textbf{Models.} InternVL3-8B, Ristretto-3B and gpt-5-nano ($n=3$): two open
models and one API model. This is a case study sized to validate the audit
machinery, not a leaderboard; leave-one-model-out ranges are reported
throughout and are wide (Table~\ref{tab:pools}).

\textbf{Scoring.} The judged arm uses \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} as the
answer-extraction judge, identical for full and blind runs; the exact arm
parses option letters directly from predictions with no model in the loop.
Consensus, $\kappa$, position and distractor detectors use the exact arm by
construction. Both arms are reported wherever they can differ.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Every run records its git commit, command-line
arguments and generation parameters in a manifest; the audit is
deterministic given the artifacts and a fixed permutation seed. The audit
layer, the prediction artifacts for both benchmarks, and the generated
reports accompany this submission; Appendix~\ref{app:repro} gives the exact
commands that regenerate every number and figure in this paper.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{A curated benchmark still carries a text-solvable excess}
\label{sec:res-textonly}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_visual_dependency.pdf}
  \caption{The text-only signal on MMStar. \textbf{(a)}~Model-averaged full
  against blind accuracy per question (judged arm, jittered; three models
  give the $4\times4$ grid). Questions on the diagonal gain nothing from the
  image. \textbf{(b)}~The observed 20.2\% share against 999 item-shuffled
  permutations: the null centres at 17.0\%, leaving a $+3.2$\,pp excess that
  no permutation reaches ($p=0.001$). \textbf{(c)}~The no-gain set is not
  uniform---on 164 of 303 questions every model is wrong with and without
  the image, so the honest reading is \emph{zero marginal value of the
  image}, of which genuinely text-answerable items are one part.}
  \label{fig:vd}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:vd} and Table~\ref{tab:pools} give the headline result. The
observed text-only share is 20.2\% (Wilson 95\% CI $[18.2, 22.3]$); the
permutation null predicts 17.0\%; the excess is $+3.2$\,pp at $p=0.001$, the
floor of a 999-permutation test. The excess survives every
leave-one-model-out pool ($+3.6$ to $+7.7$\,pp, each $p=0.001$) and its
split-half reproducibility is $\pm1.5$\,pp. Normalizing by the null is what
makes the number stable: the raw share varies by 14.2\,pp across pools, the
excess by 4.6\,pp.

Two readings of this result must be kept apart, and the per-item composition
decides between them (Figure~\ref{fig:vd}c). Of the 303 no-gain questions,
164 are items on which every model is wrong both with and without the
image---evidence of shared failure, not of text-solvability. Only 26 are
answered correctly by all three models blind. The claim we make is therefore
about zero marginal value of the image for this pool, and the excess
quantifies how much of that is more than marginal-rate arithmetic predicts.
We also read the result as evidence that MMStar's curation largely worked:
unfiltered suites show blind accuracy tens of points above chance
\citep{mmstar2024,mmevalpro2024}, whereas the residue here is 3.2 points
above the matched null. The audit's role is to measure what survives
curation, cheaply and per item.

\subsection{The two pathologies live in different places}
\label{sec:res-strata}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_strata.pdf}
  \caption{Per-category audit of MMStar (250 items per category), sorted by
  text-only excess. \textbf{(a)}~The excess concentrates where world
  knowledge can substitute for perception and vanishes in coarse perception.
  \textbf{(b)}~Unanimous label-error candidates order themselves almost in
  reverse, peaking in the perception categories. The two detectors flag
  different parts of the benchmark; six categories moving coherently also
  act as internal replication.}
  \label{fig:strata}
\end{figure}

Stratifying by MMStar's six official categories turns one number into a
pattern (Figure~\ref{fig:strata}). The text-only excess concentrates where
world knowledge can substitute for perception: math ($+6.6$\,pp) and science
\& technology ($+7.5$\,pp). Coarse perception shows none ($-1.5$\,pp)---blind
models cannot fake seeing. Unanimous consensus conflicts distribute the
other way around: they peak in the perception categories (11.6\% coarse,
14.4\% fine-grained) and are lowest in math and reasoning (4.4--6.8\%), with
science \& technology intermediate (10.4\%). The two detectors are not
renamings of one defect; they flag largely disjoint subsets with opposite
category profiles.

\subsection{Label-error candidates}
\label{sec:res-consensus}

The consensus detector flags 23.9\% of MMStar items at some tier; the tier
that matters is the unanimous one. On 134 questions (8.9\%, Wilson 95\% CI
$[7.6, 10.5]$) every parseable model answer is the same option and it is not
the gold label; on 117 of those all three models answered in parseable form,
and the remaining 17 rest on two, because the abstention rule excludes rather
than guesses. Fleiss' $\kappa$ over raw extracted answers is 0.495 (0.544
when items with abstentions are excluded), so unanimity is not the baseline
behaviour of this pool. The rate is of the same order as the
annotation-error rates reported for MMStar by the independent fallibility
analysis of \citet{infodensity2025}, which did collect fresh human labels.

We deliberately call these \emph{candidates}, and the reason is measurable
in our own pool. Errors of different models are correlated
\citep{kim2025correlated}: when two of our models are both wrong, they pick
the same wrong option in 64--74\% of cases, against 26--27\% when the
item-to-item pairing is destroyed by the same shuffle that defines our null.
Unanimity therefore cannot adjudicate a gold label on its own---it
concentrates human review, which remains the arbiter. The audit layer ships
the flagged list with per-question model votes for exactly that purpose; a
human verification campaign over a stratified sample is the natural next
step and is not part of this paper's claims.

\subsection{Removing the LLM judge changes almost nothing, for a reason}
\label{sec:res-judge}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_robustness.pdf}
  \caption{Judge dependence of the audit on MMStar. \textbf{(a)}~Category
  shares under the \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} judge and under exact letter
  matching, on identical predictions: every share moves by at most 2.1\,pp
  and no conclusion changes. \textbf{(b)}~The disagreement has a single
  source---for the two open models the judge and the letter parser score
  every item identically, while gpt-5-nano, whose answers are unparseable on
  10\% of items, accounts for all of it.}
  \label{fig:robust}
\end{figure}

Audit claims should not silently inherit an LLM judge
\citep{rosenthal2025unexplored}. Four of six detectors never use one
(Table~\ref{tab:detectors}). For the two that do, we recomputed everything
under exact letter matching on identical predictions
(Figure~\ref{fig:robust}a): category shares move by at most 2.1\,pp
(text-only 19.8\% against 20.2\%; visual-dependent 20.5\% against 22.5\%;
supplement 53.6\% against 52.3\%; conflicting 6.1\% against 5.0\%), and
nothing in Sections~\ref{sec:res-textonly}--\ref{sec:res-consensus} changes.

The instructive part is where the two arms disagree. 122 of 1{,}500 items
(8.1\%) change visual-dependency category between arms, and every one of
them traces to gpt-5-nano: the judge and the letter parser score that model
differently on 229 model-item pairs, and the two open models on none at all
(Figure~\ref{fig:robust}b). The mechanism is prosaic---gpt-5-nano answers in
free-form prose on 10\% of items, which a regular expression cannot read but
a judge can. The judge is thus a robustness arm rather than a dependency:
for models with canonical outputs exact matching suffices, and for models
without, the audit report can say so per model instead of averaging the
discrepancy away.

\subsection{Where the detectors stop working: a binary-format probe}
\label{sec:res-binary}

We ran the same audit on HallusionBench \citep{hallusionbench2024}, whose
951 items are yes/no questions, expecting trouble---and measuring it. With
two answer options blind guessing sits at 50\%, unanimity is cheap, and the
multiple-choice consensus machinery does not apply at all: option letters do
not parse and the exact arm reports abstention everywhere. The text-only
excess is still large and significant ($+5.4$\,pp over an 18.5\% null,
$p=0.001$), but the decisive calibration test fails. Ranking items by their
continuous visual gain recovers HallusionBench's own visual-dependent
against visual-supplement design labels at AUC 0.49, that is, at chance
(Appendix~\ref{app:binary}). One directional signal survives and matches the
benchmark's intent: the excess is 2.3 times larger on design-VS items
($+7.7$\,pp) than on design-VD items ($+3.3$\,pp). We conclude that the
current detector suite is calibrated for $k$-way multiple choice and treat
binary formats as out of scope---a measured boundary rather than a footnote,
since an audit tool that does not state where it is invalid invites the same
over-reading it exists to prevent.

\subsection{Auditing the audit}
\label{sec:res-selfaudit}

Audit pipelines can manufacture their own findings, so we checked ours
against the known failure modes. \emph{No-op perturbation}: the blind run
strips images centrally, blind transcripts contain model statements that no
image is present, and blind accuracy falls towards the option prior in the
perception categories. \emph{Extraction artifacts}: the judged-against-exact
arm (Section~\ref{sec:res-judge}) measures precisely this and attributes all
discrepancy to one model. \emph{Unfaithful scoring}: the judged arm is
VLMEvalKit's native protocol, unmodified. \emph{Broken pairing in
uncertainty}: the permutation null is item-paired by construction---only the
full-to-blind linkage is destroyed. \emph{One scalar, two readings}: every
headline is disaggregated (category strata, per-pool tables, and the
164-of-303 composition in Figure~\ref{fig:vd}c). One bug we did catch and
fix is reported in Section~\ref{sec:method-inputs}: back-filling gold labels
into model answers had suppressed unanimous conflicts from 134 to 12, and
the layer now refuses to do it.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

(i)~Three models. Consensus unanimity among correlated models
\citep{kim2025correlated} is weak per-item evidence---we measure 64--74\%
same-wrong-option agreement---so the detector produces candidates for review
rather than verdicts, and the leave-one-model-out ranges in
Table~\ref{tab:pools} show how much three models leave open. (ii)~The
label-error candidates in this paper are unverified by humans; the precision
of the consensus detector is therefore unmeasured, and the 8.9\% figure is a
candidate rate, not an error rate. (iii)~Unparseable predictions become
abstentions, which bakes each model's refusal policy into per-item
statistics. (iv)~A blind-run gain conflates language priors, answer-option
statistics and training-data contamination; the audit bounds their sum and
cannot separate them (cf.\ \citealp{mmdetect2024}). (v)~The
visual-dependent and supplement categories depend on the threshold $\delta$;
we headline only threshold-free quantities and publish the sweep
(Appendix~\ref{app:sweep}). (vi)~Binary yes/no formats are out of
calibration, as measured in Section~\ref{sec:res-binary}. (vii)~One
benchmark is audited in depth; the claims are about the audit machinery, and
its base rates should not be extrapolated to other suites without running
it.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An evaluation toolkit already stores what a benchmark audit needs. Adding
one text-only rerun and a statistics pass turns those artifacts into
per-item findings with uncertainty attached: on MMStar, a $+3.2$\,pp
text-solvable excess concentrated in knowledge categories, and 134 unanimous
label conflicts concentrated in perception categories. The practical
implication runs through leaderboards: a model delta smaller than a
benchmark's measured artifact rates should not be read as a capability
difference, and an audit report costs little enough to ship alongside every
score. We are extending the pool to six models, running the human
verification the consensus detector calls for, and sweeping the remaining
VLMEvalKit benchmarks; the layer, artifacts and reports are released so that
others can do the same.

\section{Reproducing every number in this paper}
\label{app:repro}

The audit consumes only the per-model result files that VLMEvalKit writes
for a normal evaluation, plus the matching blind-run files. With those in
place, two commands regenerate all reported statistics and all figures:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
python scripts/audit\_significance.py \textbackslash\\
\hspace*{1em}--full  <model>\_MMStar\_gpt-4o-mini\_result.xlsx (one per model) \textbackslash\\
\hspace*{1em}--blind <model>\_MMStar\_gpt-4o-mini\_result\_blind.xlsx \textbackslash\\
\hspace*{1em}--models InternVL3-8B Ristretto-3B gpt-5-nano \textbackslash\\
\hspace*{1em}--strata-column category --perms 999 --seed 7 --out <report dir>
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
python scripts/audit\_paper\_figures.py \textbackslash\\
\hspace*{1em}--full ... --blind ... --models ... --out paper/figures
\end{quote}

The blind artifacts themselves come from a normal evaluation invocation with
the \texttt{--blind} flag, which is the only step that requires inference:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
python run.py --data MMStar --model <model> --blind
\end{quote}

The figure script imports its detector logic directly from the statistics
script, so a figure cannot disagree with a reported number. Both scripts
depend only on \texttt{pandas}/\texttt{openpyxl} (plus \texttt{matplotlib}
for figures) and do not import the toolkit itself, so an audit can be
re-run on a machine that cannot host the models. Randomness enters only
through the permutation seed (default 7), which is recorded in the report.

\paragraph{Answer extraction (exact arm).} A prediction is mapped to an
option letter by three ordered rules: a bare letter, possibly punctuated; a
letter followed by a delimiter at the start of the string; or a letter
following the words ``answer'' or ``option''. Anything else becomes the
abstention sentinel \texttt{Z}, which is excluded from consensus votes.
This mirrors the toolkit's own extractor, so the audit does not introduce a
second, divergent notion of what a model answered.

\paragraph{Permutation null.} For each of 999 permutations, one random
permutation of question ids is drawn and applied to the blind hit vectors of
\emph{all} models simultaneously. This preserves each model's marginal blind
accuracy and the correlation between models' blind answers, and destroys
only the per-question pairing between full and blind runs. The text-only
share is recomputed on each permuted dataset with the identical category
rule; the reported excess is observed minus null mean, and the one-sided
$p$-value is $(g{+}1)/(N{+}1)$, where $g$ counts permutations with a share
at least as large as observed. Stratified nulls repeat the procedure within
each category. For the split-half check, questions are randomly halved, the
excess is computed in each half, and the median absolute difference across
resplits is reported: $\pm1.5$\,pp on MMStar and $\pm2.1$\,pp on
HallusionBench.

\section{Threshold sensitivity of the visual-dependent share}
\label{app:sweep}

The visual-dependent share depends strongly on its definition, which is why
the paper never headlines it. On MMStar (judged arm), the strict all-or-none
definition flags 338 items (22.5\%), while a majority definition---at least
two of three models correct with the image and at most one without---flags
822 items (54.8\%). On HallusionBench the same pair is 140 (14.7\%) against
478 (50.3\%). The text-only category has no threshold at $n=3$, since the
accuracy grid makes it exact equality, and the unanimous consensus count has
none at any $n$; those are the quantities the paper reports.
